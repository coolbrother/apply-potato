CLASSIFICATION GUIDELINES:

stage_done:
- A RECEIPT for something already finished, not an invitation to start it. Judge this by
  the TENSE, never by who sent it. Past tense about the applicant's own action is the
  whole signal: "Assessment completed", "Thanks for taking", "Thank you for completing",
  "You have completed", "Your submission has been received", "Thank you for submitting
  your On-Demand Interview", "we've received your video interview".
- Do not require a recognised platform. These arrive from whichever service the employer
  happens to use, and there is always one more — CodeSignal, HackerRank, HireVue,
  Codility and Modern Hire have all appeared, and the next will not be on any list. An
  unfamiliar sender saying "thank you for submitting" is still a completion.
- The company is usually named in the subject or body even when the sender is a platform.
  Extract it as normal.
- Set "stage_completed":
    "OA"        a coding assessment, online test, game-based assessment, or a recorded
                one-way video interview ("on-demand", "on-demand web/video", HireVue or
                Modern Hire style). These are the first round, sent to everyone who
                applies, so they belong with the assessment rather than the interviews.
    "Phone"     a phone screen that has taken place
    "Technical" a LIVE technical round with an interviewer — an onsite, a virtual
                pairing session, a coding interview. Not a recording submitted to nobody.
- An assessment INVITATION is "oa", never "stage_done", however friendly the wording.
  "Reminder to complete your assessment" is still an invitation — nothing is finished.
  Neither is an account or onboarding mail from the platform: "Welcome to Codility"
  arrives when the account is created, not when the test is submitted. That is "unknown".
- Results are not completions. "Assessment Results", an outcome, or anything that moves
  the candidate forward or ends the process is "offer", "rejection", or the next stage.

A CUE COUNTS WHEREVER IT APPEARS. The phrases listed under each category below are
things to look for in the subject OR the body, and the body is the stronger evidence
of the two. Many employers send every message under one generic subject — "Application
Update", "Status change", "A message about your application" — while the body says
plainly what happened. Read the body and classify on what it says; never fall back to
"unknown" merely because the subject is uninformative.

confirmation:
- Subject or body contains: "application received", "thank you for applying",
  "application submitted", "thank you for your interest in our open position"
- A first acknowledgement that the application arrived is a confirmation even when the
  subject is generic and even when the body adds that it is under review: "Thank you for
  applying to X. We are reviewing your application" is a confirmation, not "unknown".
  Being reviewed is what an application does after it is submitted; it is not a further
  stage, and no stage has been reached beyond applying.
- Usually automated, from ATS systems

oa:
- Subject or body contains: "assessment", "coding challenge", "HackerRank", "CodeSignal"
- Body contains link to assessment platform
- KEY RULE: "oa" requires something to COMPLETE — a test, assessment, or coding
  challenge the candidate is being asked to take, usually with a deadline. A link
  alone is not enough. Being given access to a site is not an assessment.
- A candidate portal, applicant account, or profile page is NOT an assessment
  platform, however it is worded. This is NOT an "oa":
    "IMC has invited you to the Software Engineer Internship Candidate Portal ...
     It's packed with helpful resources to help you learn and prepare"
  Nothing there asks the candidate to complete anything, so no stage has changed.
  It is "unknown".
- Usually has a deadline mentioned

phone:
- Subject or body contains: "phone screen", "phone interview", "recruiter call"
- Body mentions scheduling a phone call
- Usually 15-30 minutes, initial screening

technical:
- Subject or body contains: "technical interview", "onsite", "virtual onsite", "coding interview"
- Body mentions technical rounds, coding problems, system design
- Usually longer (1-4 hours), may be multiple rounds

offer:
- Subject or body contains: "offer", "congratulations", "welcome to the team"
- Body contains offer details (salary, start date, benefits)
- Positive, celebratory tone

rejection:
- Subject or body contains: "unfortunately", "other candidates", "not moving forward"
- Body is polite but negative
- Usually short, generic
