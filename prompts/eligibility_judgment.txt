You are NOT summarising the posting and NOT filling in a schema. You are answering a

{content}

    "checks": [
        {
            "dimension": "class_standing | graduation_timeline | work_authorization",
            "passes": true | false,
            "evidence": "the sentence from the posting you relied on, copied EXACTLY",
            "reasoning": "one sentence tying that requirement to the applicant"

    ],

RULES

1. Judge only these three dimensions: class_standing, graduation_timeline,
   work_authorization. Job type and season/year are decided elsewhere — ignore them
   entirely, even if the posting is for a season the applicant did not ask about.

2. "eligible" is false if and only if at least one check has "passes": false.
   Set "blocking_reason" to name that requirement, otherwise null.

3. EVIDENCE MUST BE COPIED VERBATIM from the posting — an exact substring, not a
   paraphrase, not a summary, not reformatted. A check with invented evidence is
   discarded and the job falls back to the old code path, so paraphrasing silently
   throws your answer away. If the posting says nothing about a dimension, omit that
   check entirely rather than inventing a quote.

4. Silence is not a requirement. If the posting states no class standing requirement,
   there is no class standing check to fail. Do not infer a requirement from the
   seniority of the title, the prestige of the company, or the difficulty of the work.

5. Only reject on what the posting actually requires of the APPLICANT. Descriptions of
   the role, the team, the pay, or the ideal candidate are not eligibility rules.
   "Strong programming skills" and "excellent academic background" disqualify nobody.

READING REQUIREMENTS CORRECTLY — these are the specific mistakes to avoid.

A list of degree levels is an OR, and naming a senior degree WIDENS eligibility:
  - "Current Bachelor's, Master's, or PhD students" — a Junior qualifies, via
    Bachelor's. The presence of PhD does not make this graduates-only.
  - "Pursuing a Bachelor's or Master's degree in Computer Science" — a Junior
    qualifies. The field (Computer Science) is a major requirement, not a standing.
  - "Master's or PhD students only" — a Junior does NOT qualify; no undergraduate
    degree is named.

"Graduate" as a verb or a date is not the Graduate degree level:
  - "Graduating between December 2027 and July 2028" is a GRADUATION TIMELINE. It says
    nothing about whether the applicant must be a graduate student.
  - "graduating in Fall 2027 or later", "the last requirement for you to graduate" —
    same. Only the noun sense ("graduate students", "enrolled in a graduate program")
    means the Graduate level.

Graduation windows have a direction — read the whole phrase:
  - "graduating in Fall 2027 or later" is a FLOOR. Someone graduating May 2028 is
    eligible; someone graduating May 2027 is not.
  - "must graduate by June 2027" is a CEILING — the opposite.
  - "Graduation date of December 2027 or May 2028" is a LIST of acceptable dates, not
    a range and not a deadline. Someone graduating May 2028 is eligible.
  - "between December 2027 and July 2028" is a range; May 2028 falls inside it.

Enrollment-only requirements admit any current student:
  - "must be currently enrolled", "must return to school after the internship" — these
    require being a student, and set no year level. A Junior qualifies.

Work authorization:
  - Reject only when the posting's requirement genuinely excludes this applicant, e.g.
    it requires citizenship the applicant does not have, or states it will not sponsor
    and the applicant needs sponsorship.
  - A US Citizen meets every authorization requirement. Never reject one.

WHEN GENUINELY UNSURE, PASS. A wrongly rejected job is invisible to the applicant and
lost. A wrongly passed job costs them one glance at a posting they cannot apply to.
